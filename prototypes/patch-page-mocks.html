Three ways the owner's card could read

Static mocks for the Portal map. The card at /patches/<name> was picked on the list mocks; this page settles its Manage side. Same patch, same owner, three structures. After the variants: the retired, deleted and deactivated-owner cards, an admin on someone else's card, and the deactivation and reactivation pages, which every variant shares. Nothing here is live. Buttons and fields are drawn; the real ones are plain POST forms.

What every variant shares

The card's top is fixed by the list mocks: name as heading, title as one secondary line, description with its muted actor line, Open, then the facts. The facts gain a plain-words "Who can open it" and a "Read by" row so the dependants are visible before any destructive act. What varies is where the actions live: on the card, on their own pages, or split by how reversible they are. Rollback is a pointer move, restore always lands live, and retire, delete and reassign are the acts a non-builder must not do by accident.

A

Everything on the card

The card is the whole management page. Each action is a small form in its own section, top to bottom in order of how often you'd use it, and the destructive ones sit together in an amber block at the end with their acknowledgement right there. No second page, ever. Bet: an owner wants to see and change everything in one scroll, and a form you can see is a form you understand.

B

One page per action

The card stays a dossier. Manage is a short list of what you can do, one line each with a plain sentence of consequence, and every action is its own small page at /patches/<name>/<action> with one form, one button and a way back. Bet: the card is read far more often than it is changed, and a page that does one thing is the easiest form for a non-builder to trust.

C

Edits inline, destruction confirms

Split by reversibility. What can be undone with one more click (description, who can open it, making a version current, and for admins the owner) is a form on the card. What breaks other people's tools (retire, delete) is one button each in an amber block, and the button leads to a confirmation page that lists the damage and asks for the acknowledgement. Bet: the card should feel safe to poke at, and the only time you leave it is when you are about to break something.
