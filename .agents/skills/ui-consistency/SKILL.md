# UI consistency review

Review changed page-rendering code and directly affected call sites against the rules below. Apply them when a change creates, moves, or modifies markup or styling on a served page. Do not demand unrelated repository-wide cleanup.

Three page kinds:

- **App pages**: Home, All patches, a patch's pages, Your agents, People, Integrations and their connect flows, Agent access, What's new, What you sent. `htmlPage` draws the signed-in frame: one sidebar from core's `places`, which the switcher lists too (the brand, the patch search, New patch to `/?new`, then Home, All patches, Your agents and Settings, whose People, Integrations and Agent access show under it while one of them is open, and at its foot What's new, with up to five headlines and See all changes, Tell Patchy, and the account menu). Tell Patchy's panel, a foot menu in What's new's frame, sits outside the folding menu so a page's own button with `popovertarget="tell-patchy"` can open it beside itself, its `data-kind` picking Request or Feedback; What you sent lights no place. Every role sees the same places; what a member can't change is read-only text on its page, never a disabled control. The page's place is lit with `aria-current`, its H1 is the place's name, and `htmlPage` titles it "Heading – Place – Company". Below 1024px the sidebar folds into a top bar of the brand, the search (its icon until focused) and Menu, whose popover is the same markup, so there is one nav and no script. Mod+Shift+K opens the switcher over it. Auth's `pageResponse` takes the viewer and section in app mode.
- **Doors**: sign-in, create-or-join, a beta link, device login, consent, deactivated and error pages, and Patchy's notices: one card on the canvas, never app navigation.
- **Served patches**: `renderPatchWrapper` is user content and deliberately does not compose `htmlPage`.

## The system

Every first-party page composes `htmlPage`, through `pageResponse`, and one of five templates. A page fills slots; it never styles them.

- **Styles.** `appStyles` is named chunks in `packages/core/src/styles/`, composed in cascade order from one list in `index.ts`: `fonts`, `tokens`, `base`, `canvas`, `type`, then one chunk per component family. A new family is a new chunk file and one line in that list; a page adds no CSS. Every visual value comes from the token block in `tokens.ts`: colours, the seven type roles (`--text-title` 28/34 800, `--text-title-sm` 22/28, `--text-section` 16/24 600, `--text-prose` 15/24, `--text-body` 14/20, `--text-label` 14/20 500, `--text-meta` 13/18, `--text-badge` 12/16 500, `--text-code` 13/20 mono) in four weights, spacing, control heights (`--h-control` 36, `--h-sm` 32, all 44 and 16px field text on a coarse pointer), radii 6/10/12 and the `--lift`, `--float` and `--modal` shadows. Text is Geist and Geist Mono, core's committed fonts. Titles (`--font-display`) are Cabinet Grotesk over Geist where the release has its file, which production does and the repo doesn't, so elsewhere they are Geist; never name it outside `--font-display`. Every face is served at a digest URL under `/assets/fonts/`, so every first-party policy admits `font-src 'self'`; a running patch's shell and a company look name none of them. Nothing is set in capitals. A colour, type role, corner, shadow or control height written outside the token block fails the build (see **Checks**); a value with a role and no token earns a token in `tokens.ts`, named for its role.
- **Templates** (`packages/core/src/ui/templates.ts`), each returning the `heading` and `body` a page spreads into `pageResponse`:
  - `indexPage`: header, a toolbar (segmented filter, then search or chips), the table or rows or their empty state, pagination. Max 1120px.
  - `detailPage`: parent link, the header with the object's mark, tabs, the main column and an aside that drops below on a narrow page. Max 1120px.
  - `settingsPage`: `card`s of setting rows, each its own form with its own Save, the danger card last. Max 760px.
  - `flowPage`: parent link, title, numbered sections in task order (a section still to come is a disabled fieldset), an outcome (what it can and can't do once done), the primary and Cancel. Max 640px.
  - `doorPage`: kicker, title, body, one primary and a quiet secondary, and a foot such as Not you, in the door's card.
- **Helpers** (`packages/core/src/ui/`), typed functions returning HTML; text slots are escaped, slots named as trusted HTML are not:
  - Buttons: `button`, `linkButton` and `iconButton`, kinds `primary`, `secondary` (default), `quiet`, `danger`, `danger-solid`, sizes `md` and `sm`. A submit that waits on Patchy, such as Test and connect, takes `pendingLabel` ("Testing…"): pressed, the enhancer shows it in place of the label, static, with `aria-disabled`, and turns away a second submit of its form until the page answers. The button holds room for the longer of its two labels from the start, so pressing it moves nothing. `pending` draws that pressed state, as the gallery does. The server's own guards are still what stop a repeat.
  - Fields: `field` draws label, hint, control, error in that order and hands its control the ids, so the hint and error describe it (`aria-describedby`) and a refusal marks it `aria-invalid`. A hint given in parts may hold a `HintEcho`, a live part that follows what the control holds, or its example while it is empty, such as the name in "Patches use it as attio/crm"; without script it shows what was submitted. `fieldRow` sets fields that belong together side by side, wrapping on a phone. Controls: `input`, `textarea` (`grow` for a list), `select`, `prefixInput` (a fixed prefix fused before it, a second label; `input` and `prefixInput` take `exact` for what is typed exactly, such as an address, so a phone neither capitalises nor corrects it), `secretInput` (masked; its Show waits hidden for the enhancer), `checkbox`, `radio`, `choiceGroup` (a fieldset and legend), core's `choiceCards` and `tileOptions` for choices drawn large over native radios, `switchForm` (a one-button form whose press posts the other state), `copyLine`, `copyButton` (a Copy the copy script reveals), `searchField` (a list's GET search, carrying its other filters) and `checklist` (rows of checkboxes with a description each, such as the patches to retire).
  - Feedback: `notice` (info, warning or danger, at most one a page; danger is an alert), `receipt` (one quiet line for what the last form did; there is no success notice), `badge`, `dot`, `unopenedName` and `emptyState`. `unopenedName`, or `row`'s `unopened`, draws a patch's name inside its link with the blue dot after it, which means only that it is a colleague's live patch the viewer has never opened.
  - Doors: `doorCard` is the card around a `doorPage`, which `pageResponse` draws for every door and Serving's notices draw for theirs; `verificationCode` (a code to compare, in tabular mono), `meet` (who meets whom before consent), `canList` (what it can and can't do, each line saying which) and `answerPair` (two answers of equal weight, such as Deny and Allow). Auth's `doorResponse` answers with a door, and `accountFoot` is its foot for a signed-in person: who, and Sign out, Not you? or Use another account. A form whose submit sits in the door's actions gives the form an id and the button `form`.
  - Refusals: a refusal no field owns is wrapped in `refusal` (`refusalNotice` for plain words), above the button it refused, and the page's focus starts on it; one a field owns is that field's `error`, and the field takes `autofocus`.
  - Structure: `pageHeader`, `section`, `card`, `settingRow`, `facts`, `row` and `rows` (with `href` the whole row opens it while its own buttons and menu stay pressable), `table` (named, scrolling in its wrapper on a phone; a column can hide on a phone or hide its header from sight), `tabs` (underline, between an object's pages), `segmented` (a list's filter), `chip` (a log's filter), `menu` (a native popover of links and buttons, not an ARIA menu), `dialog`, `steps` (setup only, at most three), `disclosure` (a summary that opens its parts in place, such as No agent yet?), `changeEntry` and `behindTheScenes` (What's new's one-line changes, opening in place; a permalink fragment opens one) and `idFor` for repeated rows' ids. Menus' items: `menuLink`, `menuPost`, `menuCopy`, `menuNewTab` and `menuRule`, behind `rowMenu`'s quiet ⋯. A detail page's parts: `chipMenu` (a filter chip opening its values as links), `groupedTable` (rows under group heads such as a log's days, each opening in place), `expandingTable` (any table whose rows open to more), `settingsPanel` (the Settings template inside a tab), `codeRow` and `person`. Home's: `region` (a section that is a named landmark), `attentionRow` (what broke and its one fix) and `usualTiles`. A flow's: `choiceLinks` (a choice that is a place, such as which app) and `flowOutcome`. Marks: `patchMark`, `avatar`, `agentMark`, `productMark` (another product's, such as an integration's: the product's own logo in its colours where Patchy knows the product, from core's `logos.ts`, else an icon or its letters) and `computerMark`.
- **Rules.**
  - One primary per region: blue fill is the region's main action, nothing else. Copy buttons, Connect in a catalogue and row actions are secondary.
  - The hard shadow means lifted: the primary button, the glyph, a hero mark, menus, dialogs and doors. Rows, tiles at rest, containers and marks in lists carry none. Ink edges on what you press; hairlines on what holds.
  - Success is quiet; problems are loud. Badges only for states that need a decision, always with a word. Never "Healthy".
  - Every state has a URL: tabs, filters, dialogs and confirmations are links and GET routes, and work without script. Disclosures and menus are ephemeral.
  - Focus is a 2px ink ring, never blue. Hover only under `(hover: hover)`. Disabled is avoided; read-only is text.
  - Nothing moves, nothing loops: no spinner, pulse, shimmer or smooth scroll. The only motion is a hover lift and a press of at most 120ms.
  - Doors and app pages share one canvas, the `canvas` chunk's squared paper over a blue → cream → green wash, scrolling with the page (never `fixed`). Only what is bordered, pressed or floats has a surface: the sidebar and top bar, the door card, boxes, cards, tiles, notices, controls, menus, dialogs and the switcher. Nothing else has one, so the squares show behind tables, lists, prose, a flow's sections and every title, and a row's hover there is a translucent tint. A background on anything else is a finding.
  - Phones: 16px page padding, header actions under the title, tabs scroll, asides stack after main, wide tables scroll in their own wrapper, field text 16px on a coarse pointer.
- **Dialogs** are URLs: the page's GET route renders the page with `dialog` as the app shell's `overlay`, the page behind going inert under a static scrim. Its first field, or for a destructive one its Cancel, takes `autofocus`. Without script it is the page (`@media (scripting: none)` leaves out the inert page behind and the scrim), and its Close is a link back. A dialog that is one form takes `form`, so its body and foot post together, and a refusal draws it again with the field's error. A dialog only its own actions may close, such as the Final step, leaves out its close.
- **The enhancer**, `/assets/ui.js`, which `pageResponse` loads on every page from the portal's session-free route: a dialog's focus starts on its `autofocus` control; reached by a link on this site, Escape and Close go back in history to that page, past any refused submission the dialog was drawn again for, as does Back from a refused submission, and loaded any other way they go to its own page; focus returns to the link that opened it, or its menu's button; Show toggles a secret's mask; a hint's `HintEcho` follows its field; a pressed submit with a `pendingLabel` goes pending, unless a page's own script prevented the submit, and a page restored from the back-forward cache comes back at rest; a fragment naming something inside a `<details>` opens it. Everything works without it.
- **The gallery**, `/~ui`, development only: every helper in its states with a state table per component, and one page per template plus a dialog at `/~ui/<template>`. A helper core exports arrives with its gallery entry and state table, or the build fails. The server mounts it with the persona picker, so production answers its 404. `pnpm dev shot <person> /~ui` captures it. Check a system change there first.

## Checks

`pnpm check` fails a page that styles itself. Each check names its own exceptions, and an exception added without its reason is a finding.

- **The stylesheet** (`packages/core/src/styles/rules.ts`, run over every chunk by `styles.test.ts`; `pnpm ui:report` prints the same list): no colour literal, and type, corners, shadows and controls' heights from the tokens. Drawings, meaning marks, keycaps, icons and pseudo-elements, keep their own size, corners and letter size, never their own colour or shadow. Widths, gaps, padding and rows' and bars' heights are geometry. `servedStyles`, the font faces' fallback metrics and the token block aren't read.
- **Page markup** (`pnpm lint`, the repo's own `patchy/no-page-styles` and `patchy/no-hand-drawn-components`): a page module writes no `<style>`, `style` attribute or inline style from its script, and draws no button, badge, notice or field by hand, in its markup or in the DOM its script builds; a hidden protocol input is fine. `pageResponse` and `htmlPage` take no styles. The rules hold `packages/*/src` and `apps/*/src`, leaving out core (it owns the helpers and the stylesheet), the CLI and SDK (their pages are a builder's own patch), tests, and the served wrapper's page state (`stream-status.ts`, `downloads.ts`) and `<style>` (`shell.ts`), which draw a running patch's shell on `servedStyles`. `.oxlintrc.json` holds that list with its reasons.
- **The gallery** (`ui.test.ts`): an inventory maps every helper core exports to the state table or template page that shows it, so the compiler fails a new export missing from it and the test fails a table the gallery doesn't draw. The few exports that draw nothing (the enhancer's and copy script's, the gallery's own, `idFor`) are named there.

A page writes only the system's own layout and text classes beside the helpers' output: `stack`, `cluster`, `btn-group`, `box`, `prose`, `meta`, `muted`, `strong`, `cell-name`, `row-main`, `hide-sm` and their like, all from core's chunks. A class a page invents, or a helper's anatomy written out by hand, is a finding even where no check reaches it.

Shared with the served shell: `mono`, `page-mark`, `mark`, `tag`, `shelf-label`, the What's new dot, `kbd`, the command list (`command`, `command-field`, `command-row`, `command-group`, `command-head`, `command-empty`, rendered by core's `commandList` family), `field` and `field-group`, the shell's page state (`shell-bottom`, `shell-scrim`, `glyph-sm`, `note-float`, `note-collapse`, `status-chip`, `note-inline`) and downloads (`shell-corner`, `shell-downloads`) sit in `servedStyles`, the only styles a running patch's shell carries. A test pins those bytes, so they change only on purpose, never with an app restyle. The app draws the switcher from its own `switcher` chunk in its tokens.

## Scripts on first-party pages

Never inline shell script or analytics: the one inline script is core's hashed switcher relay, which `htmlPage` and `renderPatchWrapper` put at the top of every `<head>` and Serving inserts ahead of patch code, so Mod+Shift+K never reaches page code or the browser. Every `pageResponse` page also loads the enhancer from this origin, so its CSP admits `script-src 'self'`. App pages load Tell Patchy's script, `/feedback/tell.js`, and the What's new script only while What's new has a dot to clear. App shells, company shells, a public shell rendered for a member of the patch's company and top-level notices shown to an active signed-in viewer load Patchy's external switcher, `/~shell/switcher.js` from core's `switcherTag`, ahead of the session's scripts; doors, anyone else's public shell, framed notices, notices for anyone else and `patchy dev` don't. Company shells, and a public shell rendered for a member of the patch's company, load Clerk's headless script and Patchy's external session initializer; tier 1 shells also load Patchy's external broker. A refusal served at a content URL is drawn under the frame's sandbox, so `servingHeaders` drops the app's fonts and enhancer from it (`withoutAppAssets`).

## Served patches

- Every shell loads Patchy's external `/~shell/broker.js`, on company and public pages alike; a company shell, and a public one for a member of the patch's company, also loads Patchy's external `/~shell/switcher.js` first, then Clerk's headless script from the exact configured Frontend API host and Patchy's external `/auth/session.js` initializer. Shell CSP admits no inline script but the switcher relay, by its `'sha256-…'` source, and uses exactly `frame-src 'self'` and same-origin runtime connections. Only those shells admit Clerk's configured script/connect host. Every first-party page's CSP admits the relay's source the same way.
- The internal content URL allows inline script only inside `sandbox allow-scripts allow-modals`, with `default-src 'none'`, `connect-src 'none'`, and blob/data media sources; camera, microphone and geolocation remain denied. Its document is the stored bytes with only the switcher relay inserted, first in the `<head>`; the policy takes no hash, which would switch the patch's own inline script off. Keep every frame's title and referrer policy, and escape all uploaded titles and attribute values.
- Patch code acts only through Patchy and never holds the reader's login. No analytics at either scope. Public shells remain session-free except for a member of the patch's company, whose session the shell keeps outside the patch, as company shells do.
- A running page's visible content is the sandboxed frame, with one named exception: Patchy's selected page-state elements from #385. The S-C bottom-centre reconnect/version state uses a `status-chip` pill; actionable states widen into a `note-inline` bar with the action inline and `Hide` top-right, and below 480 px the bar becomes a full-width bottom sheet. Every state carries `glyph-sm`, nothing animates, and nothing on the page moves when a state arrives. Reconnect waits two seconds. Passive notices never take focus; actions follow the frame in Tab order; Hide and dismissal return focus to the frame. New-version Not now lasts until the next publish; reload-to-keep-saving has no dismissal; Hide retains an inline Reload action. Non-suppressible stopping notices remove the frame; access denial and revocation offer no reload, while session expiry and account changes return through sign-in to a whole-page reload. Validation errors stay with patch code.
- The exception also covers the switcher, the one shell UI a person opens over a running patch, and only with Mod+Shift+K: a `shell-scrim switcher` modal dialog, the cover's flat dim with no blur, holding one `command` list anchored near the top so its field never moves. It opens at once with whatever list the tab has and never animates. Escape, the chord again, or focus leaving it closes it; focus goes back to the frame, whose relay restores the field and selection the key found, or to the shell control or cover that held it. It can open over the starting cover. Rows are plain links, so navigation still enforces access.
- The exception also covers T-1, the starting cover from #385: a `shell-scrim` modal dialog, a static dim over the whole frame with one centred `note-float`, on first open and after a suspended document resumes, delayed by two seconds. Starting is `note-info` with the elapsed time as `supporting-text` ("12 s so far"), rewritten once a second outside the live region; no spinner, bar or transition. `start_failed` turns the same note `note-warn` with Try again, says what the viewer just did did not go through, and holds through automatic bind retries until `ready`, Try again or a resume. The dialog's accessible name stays "Starting your tools"; it carries `glyph-sm`, holds focus until `ready`, ignores Escape and returns focus to the frame when it closes while visible. While the switcher is open over it, the switcher keeps focus: a cover due then waits for the switcher to close, and one that clears leaves focus where it is. Hiding the document closes it; returning starts a fresh two-second delay. It replaces the reconnecting pill. Automatic backoff and Try again request another bind; they never replay failed calls.
- The exception also covers D-1 downloads from #385: a bottom-right `shell-corner` stack of `note-info` `note-float` cards, each titled "<patch> has a file to download" with `glyph-sm`, the filename in `code` and its size, then Download and quiet Not now. This names the offering patch without claiming it created a stored or shared file. The newest card joins the top and the stack grows upward, so cards already up never move. Three offers stay visible; older offers fold under an "N more files" `status-chip` disclosure with a static chevron. Download requires a trusted click in the shell, never a frame's claim of user activation. Not now discards the offer and returns focus to the frame. Passive offers do not take focus; their actions follow the frame in Tab order. Pending files are shell-local and disappear on close or reload. Between 481 and 1364 px, where the centred page-state bar can reach the corner, the stack sits above any page state using `--patchy-status-offset`; wider, the two never meet. Below 480 px the cards become full-width bottom sheets, stacked above the page state while it is up. Arrivals never move the patch content. No continuously repainting animation.
- Generated files reuse that D-1 card, titled "<patch> made a file". Above the shell's `download.bytes` limit (encoded bytes, read from the limits registry, never written into copy by hand), the same card is refused in place: `note-refused` (danger edge on amber paper), the same title, filename and size, then an alert sentence "This file is too large to download (24.1 MB, the limit is 20 MB)." with both sizes from the card's own size format, and one plain `btn` Dismiss. It offers no Download action and retains no file bytes. It stacks, folds and sheets like any other card, and Dismiss returns focus to the frame. Stored-file titles stay "<patch> has a file to download".
- The signed-out door reuses Auth's `/login` template, not a second design or a form: one primary **Sign in** link and, when sign-in runs through Clerk, a quiet **Join the waitlist** link for the private beta. Keep it in front of the patch; request-access controls wait until owner-only sharing returns.

## CSS ownership

- A page has no CSS. Core's chunks own every rule: layout, component spacing, typography, backgrounds, borders and colours.
- Do not apply `filter` to `html`, `body`, or the page root: it also tints the embedded patch frame and user media.
- Before calling a selector dead, trace the emitted class string and any class-valued field through to its DOM sink. A helper that returns a class is not proof that it is rendered.
- Flag duplicate declarations only after comparing specificity, inheritance, and the final owning element. Textually identical declarations are not necessarily redundant.
- Inspect the emitted HTML and CSS after template interpolation, nested pseudo-elements, or attribute matching. Valid-looking source is insufficient. Flag malformed or empty emitted selectors and interpolations that silently drop a rule.
- Avoid continuously repainting animations. A never-ending pulse, shimmer, or spinner pegs the GPU on high-refresh displays.

## Scroll and frames

- Until its document's relay says hello, or a document without one loads, the frame waits `visibility: hidden` with `data-waiting`, so neither a click nor Tab can focus its empty first document, however long that takes. The relay at the top of the shell releases it, not the broker, so the release always ships with the markup and a broker from another release can't strand the frame. No deadline: a frame that never loads has nothing to reach.
- The served-patch frame owns its scrolling: `html, body { height: 100% }`, `body { overflow: hidden }`, and the iframe fills the viewport. A change that scrolls the document instead of the frame changes reader behavior and is a finding.
- Verify actual scroll behavior when changing overflow ownership or frame sizing. Comparing classes in source is not enough.

## Change discipline and evidence

- Preserve responsive geometry, frame insets, clipping, radius, and the shell's shadow tokens.
- Preserve accessibility and interaction semantics through a migration: focus-visible rings, disabled behavior, keyboard behavior, pointer cursor, `aria-*`, and coarse-pointer hit targets.
- Preserve comments that explain a browser, sandbox, or CSP constraint. They are why the exception exists.
- Prefer the smallest durable contract over a page-specific workaround or a broad abstraction with one consumer.
- Require focused tests in `render.test.ts` when a change alters rendered structure, escaping, or defaulting logic. A visual-only class change does not need one.
- Match the verification gate to the change: typecheck or focused tests for rendering contracts, emitted-HTML inspection for template and selector transformations. Do not fail a change solely because an artifact the available tools cannot produce is missing.

## Reporting

Report only concrete violations introduced by changed lines, plus pre-existing behavior the change directly worsens. Anchor each finding to the smallest line range, name the broken behavior or ownership rule rather than the preferred syntax, and state the smallest fix. With no findings, report "No UI consistency findings" on one line.
