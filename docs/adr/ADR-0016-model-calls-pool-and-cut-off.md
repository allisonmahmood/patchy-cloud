# ADR-0016 — Model calls hold the model pool and stop at their action's deadline

A tier 2 action calls a company's model role through the host ([#1489](https://github.com/allisonmahmood/patchy-cloud/issues/1489)). A model can take most of an action's 60 seconds to answer, which no other integration call does, so two things are decided here that are hard to change once patches rely on them.

**An action that calls a model leaves its action slot for the model pool, once, and holds its place there until it ends.** It moves on its first model call that has passed its first check, before it takes any other capacity; the move frees its ordinary slot and never reverses, and a refused move keeps the ordinary slot and sends nothing (`busy`, naming `tier2.models.company`, `tier2.models.viewer` or `tier2.models.replica`). The move records those three limits with their peaks in the action's event. Under today's counting, eight people on 30-second model calls let none of 100 quick actions from five others run; under the pool all 100 ran (shown on #1482). Beside it, the host holds each action to four model calls running and 200 in all, and each server process has one admission queue granting a host permit and a provider record's permit together. Each execution task holds its model calls to `models.execution.inFlight` at its callback proxy: the loader marks a model call with its operation and refuses one framed with a file, or whose arguments pass `models.ingress.bytes`, before sending it; the proxy tells a marked call from others before any refusal, refuses a declared length past that cap before taking a permit and closes the connection, takes a permit before it reads the call's body, refuses past it `busy` with nothing sent, reads no more than the cap, and gives the permit back as the call ends, the guest's request closing first included. The host counts a marked call as one of its action's attempts and takes the action's place for a running model call before it reads the body, closes that call's connection after any answer it gives it unread (an ordinary callback's stays open for the next), reads no more than the cap, and checks the mark against the operation it decodes; a call whose attempt ends between that read and the gateway still says nothing was sent. It refuses a model call that arrives without that mark, which only a task of an older release sends, as `source_unavailable` with `sent: false`, in words that task's loader can read. The refusals the task itself gave an attempt's model calls, at most 32, come back with the attempt's reply from its loader and proxy: a handler that relays one reaches the page as that refusal, named by its operation unless two operations were given the same one, and words the patch writes itself, matching no such record, stay `handler_failed`.

**A model call's fence is its action's own deadline, not the 15-second integration deadline.** Once the request is prepared, the call's final check asks the viewer's door again, then reads the role, its provider, the patch owner's authority and the provider's key from one snapshot; that key is the one sent. After that check, immediately before the request reaches the transport, a call with less than two seconds left (`models.send.floor`) is not sent, and a call waiting for capacity gives up early enough to keep that floor. The whole call, its store reads and checks included, ends one second before the deadline (`models.cutoff`), closing a sent request's connection, a target for the handler to hear why, not a guarantee. One request per call, never retried and never following a redirect.

**Four outcomes are kept apart, in `details.sent`.** `busy` with the limit that refused, nothing sent; `timeout` with `sent: false`, too little time left, nothing sent; `timeout` with `sent: true`, cut off after the request reached the transport, which the provider may still charge; and a reply lost in transit, where `sent` is absent because dispatch is unknown. Every other refusal the host gives a model call says `sent` too, those before its handler runs included. The execution task's own refusals say `sent: false` when given before the call reached the host, and carry no `sent` after, which reads as unknown; a host reply past what the proxy reads is one of those. Retrying a call that was sent, or whose dispatch is unknown, can charge again; Patchy never retries one.

## Considered options

- **Park the action while its call waits**, freeing its slot and taking one again after. Rejected: the action needs a slot back exactly when many calls finish together, and a full replica then refuses work that already spent money.
- **Keep `integration.deadline`.** Rejected: a 20-second answer, ordinary for a model, would always time out (shown on #1482).
- **Retry a refused or lost call on the host.** Rejected: a provider may have charged for it, and only the patch knows whether a second charge is worth it.

## Consequences

**Capacity is per replica.** Two replicas count separately, as actions do today. The pool's company and person limits are overridable per company; the replica's and the processes' settings are deployment configuration.

**A call past the final check may complete.** A pause, a remap, a replaced key or lost access that lands after the final check's read doesn't stop the request; one that lands before that read sends nothing.
